Design Leadership

The Leadership Lesson Nobody Teaches Designers

June 14, 2026 Karthik S Krishnan
Share on LinkedinShare on X (Twitter)

I always thought being a great designer and I have to stop acting like the best designer in the room.

One thing that pops in my mind during this transition is Kakashi. For most of his career, Kakashi was known as one of the most skilled ninjas in the village. If there was a difficult mission, he could probably solve it himself. But what made him a great leader wasn't his ability to fight. It was his ability to help Naruto, Sasuke, and Sakura become stronger than they were yesterday.

I always thought being a great designer

Jump into Figma. → Fix the screen. → Improve the layout. → Polish the details. → Ship the work.

And honestly, I measured my value by the quality of the output I created.

The transition

My job is no longer to say, "Let me fix the design." My job is to say, "Let me help my team fix the design." That's one of the hardest mindset shifts I've had to make in my career.

Yes. Learning a lot about design leadership now-a-days.

Be the protector for your team

have always measured myself by the output I designed. Now, I measure success by what my team creates. Protect my team at all costs. And I’m learning not say "yes" to everything. Setting up realistic timelines, manage expectations, push back when necessary, and safeguard both quality and team energy. Never allow your team to burn out.

Give & receive feedbacks

The biggest challenge I'm facing during this transition is giving feedback. More importantly, I'm learning that leadership is not just about giving feedback. It's about building trust within the team by understanding how each person works.

Setting Systems

The agency environment always moves very fast. Creating clear design processes, review checklists, brand guidelines, and handoff standards is becoming too essential to have a healthy craftsmanship. Good leaders build systems rather than simply shipping deliverables.

Finding balance

It is not easy to find perfection but it is necessary to find the middle ground to say, "Good enough." Not every project requires perfection. However, every project should meet the standards we have established. As a leader, I need to help my team understand where extra effort is worth investing and where it isn't.

Relationship building

I'm also learning the importance of building strong relationships with founders, marketers, copywriters, account managers, and people across the organization. Having regular 1:1 conversations and spending time with them can solve problems that weeks of reviews cannot.

I'm grateful to be part of a team that has given me the opportunity to grow into this role. They understand that I'm still new to many aspects of leadership, and they've given me the space to learn, make mistakes, and improve along the way.
